Understanding climate and who is at risk

I want to knowWhere to look
What is normal rainfall for my area, and is this year unusual?Hawaiʻi Climate Data Portal — rainfall maps, Rainfall Atlas, climate summaries and the annual climate report
Have big rain events happened here before?Climate Data Portal Extreme Events
How fast does water leave my land through plants and soil?Climate Data Portal — Evapotranspiration of Hawaiʻi
Which Oʻahu neighborhoods face flooding, heat and sea-level rise?Climate Resilience Oʻahu data and maps — Climate Ready Oʻahu map
Which communities would be hit hardest by a flood?Climate Resilience Oʻahu — Oʻahu Social Vulnerability Index, and its overlays with flooding

Why does this matter for flooding?

Knowing how much rain usually falls in your area helps you spot an unusually wet season and estimate how much water a rain garden or loʻi needs to hold. Records of past storms can help your community plan for the next one.

Dry spells matter, too. Evapotranspiration shows how quickly the land loses water. Dry conditions increase wildfire risk, and after a fire, rain can carry soil off burned slopes and increase flooding downstream.

Social vulnerability maps show where residents may have fewer resources to prepare for a flood or recover afterward. Communities can use these maps to decide where flood protection would help most and explain those priorities in grant applications.
