About the partnership

InEvalTool brings together people who manage land and water, study how restoration works, and care for places across Hawaiʻi. Together they ask what a project changes, how to measure it, and who benefits.

Strategic partners Technical partners ʻĀina group

Strategic partners

Strategic partners help decide which questions the toolkit should answer. Land managers, conservation organizations, and planners bring the decisions they face in practice, from protecting a watershed to choosing where to invest in restoration.

The Nature Conservancy in Hawaiʻi

The Nature Conservancy works with local communities to protect native forests and coral reefs. Its Hawaiʻi program connects conservation on land with the health of freshwater and coastal ecosystems.

Hawaiʻi program at nature.org

Division of Forestry and Wildlife

Part of Hawaiʻi’s Department of Land and Natural Resources (DLNR), this division manages forest reserves and supports watershed partnerships. Its forestry programs include native tree nurseries, forest stewardship, and community forestry.

dlnr.hawaii.gov/dofaw

Division of Aquatic Resources

This DLNR division manages Hawaiʻi’s freshwater and marine resources. Its programs cover stream habitats, coral reefs, fisheries, and marine managed areas.

dlnr.hawaii.gov/dar

Office of Planning and Sustainable Development

Hawaiʻi’s state planning office brings together land-use planning, coastal zone management, and statewide geographic data. These resources help planners consider how development and sea level rise affect coastal communities.

planning.hawaii.gov

Technical partners

Technical partners help turn a restoration question into something a team can measure. They advise on data, models, and indicators, including how to interpret results when observations at a site are limited.

Lauren Roth Venu

Lauren founded Roth Ecological Design International in Honolulu. Her work in ecological water design looks at how a site can capture, treat, and reuse water by working with its natural drainage.

Roth Ecological Design International

Hawaiʻi Green Growth

This statewide partnership connects community, government, and business organizations around shared sustainability goals. Through the Aloha+ Challenge, it tracks progress on priorities including freshwater, local food, and natural resource management.

hawaiigreengrowth.org

Other listed technical partners include VSACE, CSC, and HI COP.

ʻĀina group

This group brings the experience of people who care for a place into the evaluation. Their observations help explain what has changed in a loʻi, on a farm, or in a neighborhood preparing for floods, and which changes matter to the people who live there.

Hoʻokuaʻāina

In Maunawili on Oʻahu, Hoʻokuaʻāina brings families, students, and volunteers together to grow kalo. Work in the loʻi connects food production with learning and care for ʻāina.

hookuaaina.org

Kaʻala Farm

This farm and cultural learning center in Waiʻanae Valley offers hands-on programs and community workdays. Participants learn Hawaiian stewardship practices by tending the land together.

kaalafarm.org

Hui O Hauʻula

Hui O Hauʻula brings neighbors and organizations together in Koʻolauloa on Oʻahu. Its work includes disaster preparedness, food security, and a community resilience hub.

huiohauula.org
